# Cognitive Neuroscience Learning Map: From the Brain to Cognition and Emotion

Cognitive neuroscience is not about memorizing that "one brain region is responsible for one function". It is about learning to use several kinds of evidence to ask how the brain produces perception, attention, memory, decision-making, emotion, and behavior. Brain regions usually work together as networks, and the same behavior may involve several processes, so you need to understand the brain, mental functions, measurement methods, and the limits of inference together.

## A suggested five-level path

1. **100 Foundations**: neurons, synapses, neurotransmitters, the central and peripheral nervous systems, and the main lobes and subcortical structures.
2. **200 Mechanisms**: sensation and perception, attention, working memory, long-term memory, executive function, motor control, and the generation and regulation of emotion.
3. **300 Measurement**: what behavioral tasks, lesion studies, EEG/ERP, fMRI, eye tracking, and peripheral physiological signals can each answer.
4. **400 Analysis**: reaction time and accuracy, ERPs, spectra, neuroimaging statistics, and multiple comparisons and data quality.
5. **500 Integration**: placing exercise, aging, emotion, EEG, HRV, and cognitive performance into one testable research model.

## Learning, reviewing, and going deeper

- **Learning**: each time, first answer "what is this construct, how is it operationalized, and how is it measured?"
- **Reviewing**: without notes, draw the relationships among brain region, network, function, and measurement, then explain them in your own words.
- **Going deeper**: compare whether two theories make different predictions, and identify the alternative explanations a study design cannot rule out.

For the first pass, learn in this order: "basic brain structure → attention and memory → executive function → emotion–cognition interaction → EEG and experimental design".

> This is an entry point, not a lookup table of brain-region functions. When you see "a region lights up", you still have to consider the task design, the comparison conditions, the time scale, and reverse inference.

## One article a day: a 28-day emotion learning path

This series does not start from "which brain regions are happiness and sadness". It answers step by step: what emotion is, how the body and brain take part together, how we measure it, and why different theories explain it differently.

### Every article answers the same six questions

1. What is today's core question?
2. How does the plain-language version explain it with an everyday example?
3. What competing theories or different definitions appear in the professional version?
4. Which brain–body systems are involved, rather than just one brain region?
5. How do researchers measure it, and what can and cannot the methods answer?
6. Which conclusions have fairly stable evidence, and which are still disputed?

At the end of each article we keep "three recall questions, one concept map, one testable question, and APA 7th references". Finishing an article does not mean writing down everything; it means being able to rebuild the concept in your own words and marking clearly what you do not know.

# Cognitive Neuroscience Learning Map: From the Brain to Cognition and Emotion

## Core scope

This track links the brain, cognition, and emotion through four layers: "neural implementation, psychological construct, measurement model, and statistical inference". Cognitive neuroscience arose where cognitive psychology, neuropsychology, and brain imaging meet; it can break mental processes down and connect them back to the nervous system, but it cannot prove a single mental function from the location of activation alone.

| Level | Core modules | Completion standard |
|---|---|---|
| 100 Foundations | Neurons, synapses, neuroanatomy, brain networks | Can explain how information is transmitted, from cells to systems |
| 200 Mechanisms | Perception, attention, memory, executive function, action, emotion | Can distinguish constructs and state observable predictions |
| 300 Measurement | Lesions, EEG/ERP, fMRI, TMS, behavioral and physiological measures | Can choose methods by temporal, spatial, and causal questions |
| 400 Analysis | Operationalization, signal processing, GLM/mixed models, multiple comparisons | Can explain every transformation from data to conclusion |
| 500 Integration | Exercise, aging, emotion, and brain–body regulation | Can build a DAG/theoretical model and design a validation study |

## Where should emotion fit?

Emotion is not an appendix to cognition. We suggest learning in parallel about emotion generation, arousal and valence, interoception, threat and reward, emotional memory, and emotion regulation, and then examining how they change attention, inhibitory control, and decision-making. Theories still disagree about how necessary cortical and subcortical structures are, so notes should distinguish "observed results", "theoretical explanations", and "claims still to be tested".

## Writing and evidence specifications for the emotion series

Each daily article follows a six-part structure: "definition, theory, mechanism, measurement, limitations, application". The plain-language version first builds intuition from an everyday situation; the professional version compares at least two possible explanations and avoids treating subjective feelings, behavioral performance, peripheral physiological responses, and neural signals as interchangeable indicators of emotion.

Each important claim should be labeled with its level of evidence:

- **Conceptual or theoretical claims**: state which theory's definition is being used, and do not write it as a consensus across the field.
- **Associational evidence**: distinguish correlation, prediction, and causation, and do not infer a particular emotion directly from fMRI activation.
- **Mechanistic evidence**: first check whether lesion, stimulation, pharmacological, longitudinal, or multi-method studies support necessity and temporal order.
- **Applied evidence**: the effects of exercise, aging, or emotion regulation should be tied back to the study population, intervention dose, outcome measures, and uncertainty.

We suggest using NotebookLM as a tool for exploring sources and asking questions across the literature, but formal articles must still open the original papers to check the authors, study design, results, and limitations. A NotebookLM summary cannot replace verification against the original text.

## Rules for evidence and review

For every topic, keep a concept card (definition, mechanism, measurement, confounders, representative evidence) and a method card (signal source, resolution, preprocessing, model, limitations). After finishing a level, run a check with unaided recall, a concept map, and a critique of a study; you move on to the next level only when you can state a falsifiable prediction.
