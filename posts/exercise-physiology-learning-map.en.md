# Exercise Physiology Learning Map: From Acute Responses to Exercise Prescription

Exercise physiology has to answer two questions: how the body keeps working during a single bout of exercise, and why the body adapts after repeated training. When you study, do not split energy systems, muscle, breathing, and circulation into unrelated chapters; together they determine oxygen delivery, energy supply, fatigue, and recovery.

## A suggested five-level path

1. **100 Foundations**: homeostasis, ATP, the three energy systems, muscle contraction, cardiac output, and gas exchange.
2. **200 Mechanisms**: how the nervous, cardiovascular, respiratory, and endocrine systems and body temperature coordinate as exercise intensity rises.
3. **300 Measurement**: heart rate, blood pressure, blood lactate, RPE, VO₂, ventilatory thresholds, strength, and power.
4. **400 Analysis**: acute responses, chronic adaptation, measurement error, within-person change, and between-group differences.
5. **500 Application**: designing an FITT-VP exercise prescription based on goals, risk, fitness level, and recovery status.

## How to review

Pick a scenario, such as "going from rest to running at high intensity", and in order sketch the changes in neural drive, energy demand, oxygen delivery, lactate, body temperature, and perceived effort. Then compare the response to one bout of exercise with the adaptation after several weeks of training, so that the two are not confused.

> Exercise prescription is not just applying percentages. The measurement method, disease risk, medication, environment, and individual response all affect how intensity is determined; in clinical settings, follow professional assessment and formal guidelines.

# Exercise Physiology Learning Map: From Acute Responses to Exercise Prescription

## Knowledge structure

This track is organized as "demand, delivery, utilization, regulation, adaptation". Cardiovascular regulation in acute exercise involves central command, the exercise pressor reflex, and baroreceptor mechanisms, whereas repeated training causes remodeling at the organ, tissue, and molecular levels. The two should be handled with different time scales and study designs.

| Level | Content | Research link |
|---|---|---|
| 100 | Bioenergetics, muscle, cardiorespiratory, and neuroendocrine foundations | Define variables and units |
| 200 | Acute integrated responses to intensity, duration, and mode | Build mechanistic hypotheses |
| 300 | CPET, thresholds, lactate, strength, and wearable measurement | Evaluate validity, reliability, and error |
| 400 | Longitudinal adaptation, fatigue and recovery, and heterogeneity of response | Mixed models and repeated measures |
| 500 | FITT-VP, risk stratification, and advanced prescription | Turn evidence into a monitorable intervention |

## Learning outputs

Organize each system as "stimulus → receptor/signal → regulator → effector → measurable outcome". For every measurement, record the calibration, the sampling conditions, the smallest detectable change, and the physiological meaning. Before moving on to prescription, you must be able to distinguish the assumptions and limits of relative intensity, absolute intensity, percentage of peak, and threshold-based intensity.
